Stubble solutions / biochar

Can crop residue become a durable climate and agriculture asset?

Biochar can convert paddy stubble into a carbon-rich material with potential value for soil health, durable carbon removal and rural enterprise.

Explore the opportunity ↓

Executive summary

Biochar is promising. Punjab now needs evidence on the model that can work locally.

Biochar is an established carbon-removal pathway and a potential soil amendment. For Punjab, the opportunity depends on feedstock quality, residue aggregation, production technology, field performance and viable routes to revenue. A locally designed pilot should test these conditions together before scale.

01Why Biochar

One pathway, three linked uses.

Biochar can connect crop-residue management with agriculture and climate objectives. The value and scale of each benefit must still be established under Punjab’s local conditions.

01

Productive Use for Crop Residue

Converts a portion of collected paddy straw and husk into a usable material.

02

Soil Amendment

Supports soil, water and nutrient-management objectives when applied appropriately.

03

Durable Carbon Pathway

Stores carbon and can support independently verified carbon-removal claims.

04Punjab Pathway

Punjab needs an enabling pathway, not a standalone technology decision.

Five policy inputs can convert interest in biochar into investable projects, credible products and measurable outcomes. Each input requires detailed eligibility and implementation design.

StepPolicy inputNear-term outputIntended outcome
01
Recognise biochar within the Fertiliser Control Order

Define the appropriate product category, specifications and quality requirements.

Formal product standard

A consistent quality baseline and a clear route to market.

Confidence in adoption

Safer application and greater trust among farmers, buyers and institutions.

Create early demand support

Assess eligible biochar products for market-development assistance under GOBARdhan.

Supported offtake

Targeted assistance for qualifying products during market formation.

Lower commercial risk

A more stable demand base while supply chains and buyer confidence develop.

Reduce the cost of plant deployment

Assess biochar units under the Crop Residue Management Scheme and Punjab’s industrial-policy framework.

Capital and financing support

Lower upfront cost through suitable grants, concessional finance or risk sharing.

More investable projects

Improved returns and a wider pipeline near high-stubble catchments.

Simplify project approvals

Issue industry guidance and coordinate clearances through a single-window pathway.

Predictable approvals

Clear ownership, documentation and timelines across agencies.

Faster commissioning

Lower transaction costs and fewer delays for credible operators.

05
Enable carbon-market participation

Support traceability, measurement, verification, registry access and buyer discovery.

Verified carbon credits

A credible carbon asset that can be contracted with long-term buyers.

A second revenue stream

Additional income that can strengthen plant viability and support scale.

Recommended next step: test the full pathway through a Punjab pilot with named owners, verified eligibility, clear costs and time-bound milestones.

05Economic Feasibility

A biochar plant can earn through two complementary revenue models.

The commercial case can combine direct sale of biochar with sale of verified carbon-removal credits. The value, timing and risks of the two revenue streams are different.

The path forward

Build the evidence for responsible scale.

Punjab’s next phase should determine where biochar adds value within the wider crop-residue-management portfolio and what conditions are required for responsible scale.

  • Demonstrate hybrid and high-tech production pathways in local conditions
  • Undertake independent field trials with research partners
  • Build the evidence base for quality, policy and carbon-market readiness
  • Strengthen collaboration across government, industry, research and farmer groups
