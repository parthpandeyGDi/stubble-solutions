Ex-situ / Compressed biogas

Turn paddy straw into
renewable gas.

Compressed biogas, also called Bio-CNG, can convert collected residue into a CNG-quality fuel and organic manure. The technology is proven; Punjab’s challenge is making the full project pipeline work.

How it works ↓Punjab pipeline ↓Economics ↓

Executive summary

CBG offers major residue demand, but viability is made across the value chain.

Plants need dependable feedstock, timely approvals, affordable finance, reliable gas evacuation and a market for Fermented Organic Manure. Punjab can unlock the pipeline by solving these constraints together rather than treating plant approval as the finish line.

01Why CBG

One residue pathway. Three forms of value.

CBG links crop-residue management with renewable gas, organic manure and rural supply-chain income. Its climate value depends on verified feedstock diversion, methane control and responsible digestate management.

The central implementation question

How many projects become reliable buyers of straw?

A sanctioned or announced plant does not create residue demand until land, approvals, financing, construction, feedstock contracts and gas offtake are in place.

What Punjab should monitor

  • Days at each approval stage
  • Financial closure and construction milestones
  • Contracted straw and storage readiness
  • Gas evacuation and offtake status
  • Commissioned capacity and actual plant load factor

Punjab State Biofuels Policy / working draft

Turn scattered approvals and incentives into one accountable pathway.

The working policy design has considered a minimum paddy-straw share for eligible projects, time-bound single-window clearances, capital support and demand-side incentives.

Important: draft provisions and amounts should not be treated as notified benefits until the final policy and scheme rules are published.

06Binding Constraints

The CBG chain must work from feedstock to final demand.

Plant viability depends on three linked systems: reliable supply, affordable transmission and dependable demand.

Value-chain lens01Supply02Transmission03Demand
Goal

Set up and operate plants with secure land, finance and year-round feedstock.

Move CBG from the plant to the buyer reliably and at a viable cost.

Create predictable offtake for gas and market demand for FOM.

What is breaking
  • Seasonal collection and storage risk
  • Delays in land and permissions
  • Difficulty securing institutional credit
  • Weak FOM offtake
  • Limited pipeline connectivity
  • Delayed CGD infrastructure
  • Right-of-way and rental issues
  • Costly cascade transport
  • Uncertain gas offtake and realised price
  • Low CNG and PNG market penetration
  • Limited industrial demand creation
  • Underdeveloped FOM distribution
What Punjab must enable

Mapped catchments, long-term feedstock contracts, safe depots, single-window approvals and finance facilitation.

Plant-level evacuation plans, coordinated pipeline delivery, clear right-of-way processes and viable interim logistics.

Enforceable gas contracts, CBG Obligation implementation, institutional demand and quality-led FOM markets.

Intended result

Plants commission on time and receive consistent feedstock.

Produced gas reaches the market without eroding margins.

Plants operate at stable load with two bankable revenue streams.

07Punjab Pathway

Move projects from permission to performance.

Five coordinated actions can convert the pipeline into dependable residue demand and operating capacity.

ActionImmediate outputIntended outcome
01Finalise a clear State Biofuels Policy

Align state provisions with the latest national scheme and define eligibility, incentives and accountable institutions.

Investment framework

Developers and lenders can assess support and obligations.

Stronger project pipeline

More credible projects reach financial closure.

02Operate a single-window tracker

Set stage-wise timelines, auto-escalation and a fortnightly review of stuck applications.

Visible bottlenecks

Every pending decision has an owner and due date.

Faster commissioning

Lower delay cost and fewer stranded approvals.

03Plan feedstock by catchment

Map biomass, assign realistic catchments and enable long-term contracts with aggregators and farmers.

Bankable supply plan

Plants can demonstrate quantity, cost and traceability.

Reliable straw demand

Farmers and aggregators gain a predictable market.

04Link plants to gas infrastructure

Coordinate CGD, pipelines, right-of-way and interim cascade logistics at project level.

Evacuation plan

Each plant has a feasible route from compression to buyer.

Higher plant utilisation

Gas production can translate into realised revenue.

05Build the FOM market

Support quality standards, demonstrations, institutional demand and organised distribution.

Structured offtake

More digestate becomes a saleable product.

Stronger plant economics

A second revenue stream reduces dependence on gas alone.
